# Pacing, Rhythm & Breathing Room

> Cross-cutting reference on time: how fast to cut, when to hold, and how to use
> the music's beat grid. Applies to every project. Builds on `kb/fundamentals.md`.

## Pacing is contrast

The single most important pacing idea: **pace is relative.** Fast only feels fast
next to slow. A wall of rapid cuts numbs; a wall of long takes drags. Design the
*shape* of the energy over the whole piece — peaks and valleys — not a constant rate.

- Shorten shots approaching tension/climax; lengthen for emotion and resolution.
- Give every fast section a slow one to play against, and vice versa.

## Cutting to music (the beat grid)

Run `analyze-music <file>` → `bpm`, `beats_seconds`, `downbeats_seconds`, `sections`.

- **Place cuts on `beats_seconds`.** A cut a few frames off the beat reads as sloppy.
- **Big shot changes on `downbeats_seconds`** (bar starts) — they carry more weight.
- **Structural changes on `sections` boundaries** (energy low→mid→high).
- **Cut frequency by section energy:** low = every 4–8 beats; mid = every 2–4; high =
  every 1–2. The *escalation* is what the audience feels as "build."
- Syncopation (cutting deliberately between beats) is an advanced flavor — use rarely
  and on purpose.

## Breathing room (the seconds between things)

Breathing room is deliberate space — pauses, holds, silence — that lets the viewer
absorb and feel. Removing all of it is the most common amateur mistake; it makes an
edit feel anxious and robotic.

**Where to leave air:**
- After a strong/emotional line or moment — hold ~0.8–1.5s; the pause *is* the emphasis.
- Before a big reveal/answer — the anticipation has value.
- At section/topic transitions — a beat of held shot or ambience signals "new idea."
- On laughs/reactions — let them play; don't cut on the first frame of a smile.
- At the end — let the final moment breathe before cutting out.

**Rule-of-thumb durations:**
| Situation | Air |
|---|---|
| Conversational pause between thoughts | ~0.3–0.6s |
| Emphasis / emotional hold | ~0.8–1.5s |
| Comedic timing (before & after a punchline) | ~0.5–1.0s each |
| Dramatic / heavy moment | up to 2–3s when earned |

**The test:** watch and breathe with the cut. Feel rushed → too little air. Attention
drifts → too much. Vary it: tighten the informational parts, open the emotional ones.

## The trim-the-fat pass

First assemblies are always long. Tighten by removing dead air before/after lines,
redundant beats, and anything the audience already understood. Then, separately, add
back the *intentional* breathing room above. Trimming and breathing room are two
different passes — don't conflate "remove filler" with "remove all pauses."
