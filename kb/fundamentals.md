# Editing Fundamentals — The Award-Winning Editor's Core

> Universal principles applied to **every** project before any genre rules.
> Read this first, then the doc for the specific project type. When they
> conflict, the project-type doc wins (it's more specific).
> Companion: `kb/editing-craft.md` covers advanced techniques (cool cuts, ramps).

## The one-sentence job

You are not arranging clips — you are **directing the viewer's attention and
emotion through time.** Every cut answers: *what does the audience need to see or
feel next?* A cut that doesn't serve that is wrong, however clean.

## The Rule of Six (why one cut beats another — Walter Murch)

Weight cuts top-down; sacrifice from the bottom:

1. **Emotion (51%)** — preserves/advances what the audience should feel.
2. **Story (23%)** — advances narrative or understanding.
3. **Rhythm (10%)** — happens at a moment that feels right.
4. **Eye-trace (7%)** — respects where the viewer is looking.
5. **2D plane / stage line (5%)** — keeps screen geography coherent.
6. **3D continuity (4%)** — physical position matches across the cut.

A cut that breaks continuity but lands the emotion is usually right. A
continuity-perfect cut that kills the emotion is usually wrong.

## Cut motivation (when to cut)

- **Cut on motion / action** — a cut hidden in a movement (head turn, reach, step)
  is nearly invisible. Cutting on a static frame draws attention to the cut.
- **Cut on the blink / beat / breath** — natural pauses are the seams nobody feels.
- **One idea per shot** — when a shot has delivered its single idea, cut.
- **Trim the fat** — first assemblies are always too long. Remove dead air before
  and after lines, redundant beats, anything the audience already understands.

## Audio is half the edit

- **Audio leads picture.** The ear accepts a cut the eye hasn't seen yet:
  - **J-cut** — next shot's AUDIO starts before its picture (pulls you forward).
  - **L-cut** — current shot's audio continues over the next picture (smooths).
  Use these constantly on dialogue/interview/VO; they make edits invisible.
- **Protect the bed.** A continuous music/ambience bed under cuts hides them and
  creates flow. Silence between cuts exposes every edit.
- **Levels:** VO/dialogue on top (~-12 to -6 dBFS peaks); music ducks under speech
  (~-18 to -24 dB below VO); SFX punctuate. Detail in `kb/audio.md`.

## Pacing is contrast, not speed

- Fast everywhere exhausts; slow everywhere bores. Earn fast with slow.
- Shorten shots toward tension/climax; lengthen for emotion and resolution.
- With music, respect the beat grid (`analyze-music`, `kb/pacing-and-rhythm.md`).

## Screen grammar (continuity)

- **180° rule / stage line** — stay one side of the action so screen direction
  holds. Cross only via a cutaway, neutral shot, or on purpose.
- **Eye-trace** — match the point of interest's screen position across a cut so
  the eye doesn't jump.
- **Match action** — overlap an action a few frames across two angles so it reads
  continuous.
- **Jump cuts** — avoid unless stylistic (vlog/talking-head tightening); then make
  them obviously intentional and rhythmic.
- **Outfit/hair/location change = new scene.** Don't intercut different sessions
  as one continuous moment (relaxes for recaps/montages where time jumps are
  expected).

## Shot grammar (what each shot is for)

- **Wide/establishing** — orient the viewer; open a scene/section.
- **Medium** — workhorse for dialogue and action.
- **Close-up** — emotion, detail, emphasis. Your most powerful tool; spend it on
  moments that matter.
- **Insert/cutaway/B-roll** — cover edits, compress time, show what's discussed,
  hide jump cuts. Backbone of recap/montage work.

## Transitions — restraint signals skill

- **Hard cut is the default and usually correct.** Award work is mostly hard cuts.
- **Cross-dissolve** = time passing / dreamy softening. Sparingly.
- **Dip to black** = chapter break / strong time gap.
- Whips, flashes, zoom transitions = spice: a few per piece, beat-motivated.
  Overuse reads as amateur.

## Color & exposure continuity

- Must match across a cut within a continuous scene. A temperature/brightness jump
  reads as a mistake — fix it or hide it with a cutaway. See `kb/color-continuity.md`.

## Finishing discipline (separates pro from rough)

1. **Watch it through as a viewer**, not an editor. Where attention drifts, cut.
2. **The opening earns the rest** — first 3–5s decide if anyone keeps watching.
   Lead with your strongest hook.
3. **The ending lands the feeling** — resolve, then get out. Don't trail off.
4. **Render frames at cut boundaries** (`sequence-render-frame`) and check framing,
   color, composition before declaring done. Self-review is mandatory.

## Self-review checklist (run before reporting "done")

- [ ] Opening hooks in the first 3 seconds?
- [ ] Every cut motivated (emotion → story → rhythm)?
- [ ] Dialogue/VO edits softened with J/L cuts?
- [ ] Cut rhythm respects the music beat grid?
- [ ] No color/exposure jumps at cut boundaries?
- [ ] Dead air trimmed; no shot overstaying its one idea?
- [ ] Audio bed continuous; levels balanced (VO on top, music ducked)?
- [ ] Ending resolves rather than trailing off?
